Congress

Under the STOCK Act (2012), members of the House must report stock, bond and option trades by themselves, their spouses (SP), joint accounts (JT) and dependent children (DC) within 45 days. This page reads every electronic Periodic Transaction Report filed with the Clerk of the House for this year and last, and refreshes weekly.

  • Amounts are ranges ($1,001–$15,000 up to over $50 million), not exact values.
  • Reports lag by up to 45 days, and some members file late, so these are not real-time trades.
  • Paper filings are scanned images and are linked but not parsed.
  • The Senate publishes reports only behind a terms-of-use page on efdsearch.senate.gov, so Senators are not included yet.

Famous investors

Fund managers with more than $100 million in US equities file Form 13F with the SEC within 45 days of each quarter end. It lists their long positions in US-listed shares and options at quarter end, but not short positions, cash, most non-US holdings or trades within the quarter. Changes are worked out by comparing the latest two filings. A filing flagged "old" means the manager hasn't filed recently, for example because the fund has closed or now files under a different name.

Jim Cramer and other commentators

Television and social-media commentators have no disclosure duty, so there is no official record of their picks. Tracking them means collecting what they say on air or online, which needs a licensed data feed or manual curation. This page sticks to legally filed disclosures.

Not investment advice. Disclosures can be late, amended or incomplete; always check the linked filing.
