# JDP-I entity layer ("data lake")

One record per person, place, polity, period, site, war, party, election or idea that appears anywhere in JDP-I, keyed by its **Wikidata ID** (e.g. John Mearsheimer = Q461830). It powers the Master Index (`encyclopedia/`) and the ◈ links added across every module.

## How it fits together

```
module data ──► adapters.mjs ──► build.mjs ──► lake/data/ ──► Master Index (Explorer + entity pages)
 (Retrospective,   mentions:      resolve IDs,     static      └► jdpi-entities.js linker on every module page
  calendars,       key + link     Wikidata facts,  shards
  battlefields,    + context      tags, summaries
  Kardashev,       + tags
  Investor Impersonator,
  Learning,
  Invention Timeline,
  Bibliotheca,
  Kino,
  National Analysis,
  Volatility)
```

- **Adapters** (`scripts/adapters.mjs`): each module reports what it mentions as `{ key, label, module, url, date, context, tags }`. The key is a Wikipedia title, a Wikidata ID, or a module ID that Wikidata can resolve, such as a Congress Bioguide ID; a Wikidata ID wherever the module's data has one. `module` is the submodule's id in `registry.json`, and `url` the exact place in JDP-I, written as that page reads its own address. `ADAPTER_MODULES` lists the submodules each adapter files its mentions under.
- **Build** (`scripts/build.mjs`, with its rules for what Wikidata and Wikipedia send back in `scripts/resolve.mjs`):
  - Resolves every key to a Wikidata ID. A key that resolves to a Wikimedia disambiguation page (P31 Q4167410 or one of its subclasses, such as Q22808320, a human name's) is left out, with a warning naming the module and key, so the module can link the right article ("David Einhorn" is a list of namesakes; Auto Trader now links "David Einhorn (hedge fund manager)"). A related item that is one gets no page.
  - Asks Wikidata in batches of 50; a batch it answers with an error (one ID it refuses fails them all) is split and asked again, so no related name is left a bare QID. A label falls back from English to the article's title, the multilingual label, the module's own name, the last build's label and another language.
  - Reads each property's best statement: the preferred one, else the first of normal rank, never a deprecated one (`scripts/dates.mjs` bestClaim).
  - Fetches facts, plus one level of related entities (parties, ideologies, movements, countries, conflicts), so those get pages too. Dates are kept at Wikidata's precision, the year first and signed (`-0356-07-20` is 20 July 356 BC), with `fp` for a precision coarser than a year (8 a decade, 7 a century) and `fc` for a date the source marks circa; the page shows them as "20 July 356 BC", "15th century", "c. 1500" (`scripts/dates.mjs`).
  - Gives each record one type, from Wikidata’s classes (P31) by the rules in `scripts/types.mjs` (typeOf), and names it in the record’s "Type:" tag by `TYPE_NAMES` there: Person, Election, War or conflict, Political party, Idea or school of thought, Place or state, Polity, Period, Site, Series, Organisation, Event, Topic. Four came on 21 November 2026 (MI-12, MI-13, MI-07), each from the classes read from Wikidata that day:
    - **Polity**: a state that no longer exists (an empire, a kingdom, a dynasty’s state, a caliphate, a city-state, a confederation, a colony, a former country). A historical country, a Chinese dynasty, a polis and the like by their class; a colony, an empire or a republic by its class with the date Wikidata says it was dissolved (P576), or the end time (P582) Wikidata gives a military occupation instead (the Ukrainian occupation of Kursk Oblast; a period’s end time does not count). A state that exists today stays a place (a sovereign state or a country with no date of dissolution, whatever older class Wikidata also gives it: the United States is also a "historical unrecognized state"), and so does a city lived in today (Venice, Hong Kong), a region or a territory.
    - **Period**: a named span of history (a historical period, an era, an archaeological age or period). A polity’s classes come first (the Zhou and Ming dynasties are periods in Wikidata too), and so do a war’s, a war word in the label (the Cold War stays a conflict), and a revolution’s (the French Revolution stays an event). A movement is a period only where Wikidata classes it as one: the Renaissance and the Age of Enlightenment, a cultural movement there, stay ideas.
    - **Site**: an archaeological site, a monument or a building of historical note (the Great Pyramid, the Pantheon, Hagia Sophia), not a museum, a university or a city lived in today; a polis that is also an archaeological site is a polity (Classical Athens).
    - **Series**: an entry that is only a grouping of others, every class it has a series (a series of wars, the events of a year, a series of works): the Balkan Wars, "2006 in Iceland". A war Wikidata also classes as a war, or that VM holds as one by its label, stays a war (the Napoleonic Wars, the War in the Sahel); its members come under MI-07.
  - Turns facts into tags: nationality, occupation, field, movement, ideology, party, position, conflict and century. The century comes from the first of birth, date, start and inception Wikidata gives, and only from a date at least as precise as a century (`scripts/dates.mjs` centuryTag): Moses, born in the 2nd millennium BC, has no "20th century BC". It adds module tags and the Learning courses' hand-made tags (`scripts/thinkers.mjs`), in the discipline's words (the canon review's table D3, 18 November 2026): **School** ("School:Realism" and its branch "School:Offensive realism", two tags; the schools of economics named as their entries), **Ideology** (named as the ideology, "Ideology:Marxism", and spelt as Wikidata's, so a value it also gives appears once), **Field**, **Theory** (a theory a thinker is known for), and an occupation or office under Occupation or Role only where the entry does not already carry it there (`adapters.mjs` mentionTags). **Political position** holds only the Political Briefing's five positions (Far left, Left, Centre, Right, Far right): the Politics course unit that teaches a thinker or school as its own gives it, and the Realists, a school of international relations, carry none. Such a position is VM's own placement, not a source's, and says so: the record keeps a note on the tag (`tn`, `scripts/thinkers.mjs` POSITION_NOTE), which the page shows beside the tag and, through `index.json` `notes`, in the rows and the filter's heading, until a sourced position is added beside it. The groups "School or tag" and "Political spectrum" went on 18 November 2026; a `--only learning` run takes them away (`ADAPTER_TAG_GROUPS`).
  - Leaves out a few Wikidata statements that rest on no real source, each listed with its reason and the day it was read (`build.mjs` HIDDEN_STATEMENTS): no tag, fact or related entry comes from them. In the same way it keeps the name a related item had where Wikidata's name for it today is not what its statements meant, again with the reason and the day (`build.mjs` LABEL_OVERRIDES: Q1227195 stays "political economist", the occupation of Adam Smith, Ricardo and fifteen others, not "Master’s Degree in Economics").
  - Shows a value once: where a hand-made School, Ideology, Field or Theory value is the same word as a Wikidata Field, Movement or Ideology value of the entry, only the hand-made one is shown (`adapters.mjs` mentionTags), so Mearsheimer has "School:Offensive realism" and not also "Field:Offensive realism".
  - Keeps each entity's first six English aliases from Wikidata, tidied by `scripts/aliases.mjs`, then the names a module's own text uses. A trailing comma goes, and so does the full stop after a regnal number ("Alexander VI."). An abbreviation keeps its stop ("With.", "Oxon."), or the Bibliotheca tagger would read it as a word at every sentence it opens. The Bibliotheca cast reads aliases by the same rule.
  - Attaches Wikipedia summaries (cached), only an article's own: none where Wikidata marks the sitelink as a redirect, where Wikipedia answers with another article's title or a disambiguation page, or where the text lists namesakes ("may refer to"); `ns` then says why (`redirect`, `disambiguation`, `empty`, `unfetched`), and the page says so: for a redirect, only that Wikipedia has no article of its own under that title (the article it leads to is often on the same subject: Sicily (island)).
  - A person's label that carries a realm after a regnal number or a comma title ("Frederik X of Denmark", "Albert II, Prince of Monaco") gets a shown `name` ("Frederik X"), which the page and the index show, but only where that short name is the entry's English Wikipedia title (Wikipedia keeps "Ferdinand II of Aragon" and "Albert II, Prince of Monaco" whole, as the short names are ambiguous), or, with no article, where no other entry is shown by it or has it as its label (`scripts/resolve.mjs` shownName, uniqueNames: no two entries share a shown name). The label, which the Bibliotheca tagger matches, stays Wikidata's and is listed among the names, and so is the Wikipedia title where it is neither ("Rainier III of Monaco").
- **Output** (`data/`), static files served by Cloudflare Pages, readable from any origin:
  - `index.json`: the search and filter index (tags, modules, one row per entity with its shown name, its tags and its first link into each module, and `notes`: the notes records give their tags).
  - `e/<n>.json`: full entity records, sharded by Wikidata number % 512. `mentions` holds each place the entity appears in once (the first 300, in module order; `places` counts them beyond that); `total` counts every mention the modules gave, a place named twice twice, and `n` the modules whose share the list shows short, so `--only` can recount.
  - `t/<n>.json`: English Wikipedia title → Wikidata ID, sharded by hash. The linker uses these.
  - `keys.json`: module keys (e.g. `congress:P000197`) → Wikidata ID.
  - `manifest.json`: counts and build time (`generated`, any run's; `fullBuild`, the last full build's, which `--only` keeps).

### What "Appears in Vitruvian Manager · N" counts

The header of an entry's appearances counts the **places** it appears in: each place a submodule links to once, a book being one place however often it names the entity (Jude, 15 November 2026, ledger BI-19 and MI-10). The list under it follows one convention for every module: the module ("Bibliotheca", "Retrospective Geopolitics"), its submodule ("Neo Book", "War Focus"), then that submodule's own place: the book, with how many times the reader marks the name, as which names, and where (each chapter, with its times, linked to the first paragraph marked there; the books have chapters and paragraphs, not page numbers); the war and the battle's figure; the country and year; the film and scene; the lesson. Module and submodule names are `registry.json`'s.
- **Linker** (`design/jdpi-entities.js`, loaded by `prp-bar.js`): adds ◈ Master Index links next to Wikipedia links, `data-qid="Q…"` and `data-entity-key="…"` elements on any JDP-I page.
- **Deep links**: Master Index links open modules through the shell as `#/<suite>/<module>?at=<url>`. The shell only accepts URLs on that module's own page.

## Adding a module

1. Write an adapter in `scripts/adapters.mjs`, add it to `ADAPTERS`, and list the submodules its mentions are filed under in `ADAPTER_MODULES`. Give each submodule its name in `MODULE_NAMES` and its place in `MODULE_ORDER` (build.mjs), and its suite in `MODULE_SUITE` (encyclopedia page).
2. Where the module shows a name without a Wikipedia link, add `data-qid` or `data-entity-key` so the linker can find it.
3. Run `npm run build:lake`, or `node lake/scripts/build.mjs --only <adapter>` to add just that module's entities to the committed `data/` (a request a second; the summaries of the entities it fetches that the cache lacks are fetched too). `--only` speaks for the adapter's `ADAPTER_MODULES`, so a submodule it no longer reports loses its mentions. A record made before `n` whose total exceeds its places cannot be recounted by an adapter that names one entity twice at one place (the Retrospective, the calendars, the Invention Timeline, Learning, Kino: `scripts/counts.mjs` REPEATING): such a run stops, naming the records, until a full build has written `n`.

`tests/master-index.test.mjs` holds the suite to this: every live submodule in `registry.json` is an adapter's or exempt with its reason, and every adapter's submodules are named, ordered, in their suite and in the committed lake. A new submodule in neither fails it, with the steps above.

Every name in the lake is also a term to the Bibliotheca tagger, which reads every label and alias when the refresh tags the books again. So an adapter feeds what its submodule shows, by Wikidata ID: a work named only in a module's sources ("The Prince" is "Prince" with its "The" dropped, Marx's "Capital" is "Capital") would be a term wherever a book uses the common word, and fail the books' tests; a title is a key only where the module's data links that article, not a display name taken for one ("Jersey" would be the island wherever Smith writes "New Jersey"). Before committing a module's first build, tag the books again in a copy of the repository (`bibliotheca/scripts/tag-terms.mjs <slug>` for each) and run `tests/bibliotheca-data.test.mjs` there.

A mention may carry `aliases`, the names the module's own text uses: the build adds them to the entity's (Bibliotheca does, so its tagger finds a book's "Duke Valentino"). The lake does not record which module gave which name, so `--only` makes an entity's names again only when the adapters it runs give names; a run of adapters that give none (Kino, National Analysis, Volatility) leaves the committed names as they are, the books' among them.

## Bibliotheca

Each book's cast (`bibliotheca/data/cast/<slug>.json`, made by `bibliotheca/scripts/cast.mjs <slug>` from Wikidata and reviewed in its header's overrides) feeds the lake through the `bibliotheca` adapter: one mention per book and entity, filed under Neo Book (`neo-classic`, in the Bibliotheca module) and linked to the first paragraph that names it there. After a build, `bibliotheca/scripts/tag-terms.mjs <slug>` tags the book again; the monthly refresh does it for every book. The tags themselves feed the lake too: every entity a book is tagged with (each paragraph's `terms`, `bibliotheca/data/books/<slug>.json`) has that book's mention, one place in the header's count, whose line gives the times the reader tags it, as which names, and the chapters it is tagged in (`w` on the mention: chapter, first paragraph tagged there, times, heading), linked to its first tag; whether it came from the book's cast or from another module (the Prince's "Milan" is the Duchy of Milan). A cast record not tagged yet counts by the cast and has no chapters to give: a book-only name (`local`) the shelf does not give back is such a record, so give it back with a shelf decision (the Prince's Scipio, Maximinus, Julian and Caesar since 16 November 2026, its "title of Caesar" left whole). Only the cast gives names. A build therefore reads the tags the last re-tag made: run it after re-tagging, as the monthly refresh does, for the counts to match the books.

A record's book-only names (`local`) feed its entity but not the name, since the name means someone else in other books (the Prince's "Cyrus", or "Naples", which there means the Kingdom). The book's shelf decision gives the name back to the entity. Every book is tagged against the one lake. So a cast record that is dropped, moved or made book-only changes the other books' terms at their next re-tag. After changing a cast, re-tag every book.

### Primary and secondary sources

Which books are primary or secondary sources on which subjects (ledger MI-14) is judged in one file, `bibliotheca/data/source-links.json`, under the rule and its wording in `bibliotheca/lib/sources.js`. Every build, full or `--only`, reads that file (`scripts/source-links.mjs`) and puts its shown links (high or medium confidence, with a tag or a note) on the records: `src` on a subject's entry (one item per book or part by another hand: the work, its title, the volumes it opens, the tag, basis, notes and evidence, each source named in words with its address, and the words the page shows), `srcFrom` on a work's own entry where the lake holds the work, and the tags "Primary source on:<subject>" and "Secondary source on:<subject>" on that work's entry, from its main text's tagged links only, each with a note (`tn`, shown beside the tag and in the Explorer's filter heading, and given by `get_entity` as `tag_notes`) saying that an AI model made the judgement and no person checked it; the dataset's file and every `query_dataset` answer from it say the same (`notes`, `dataset_notes`). A subject the book is not about is not a link: where a study raised one, the file lists it under `setAside` with the reason. No tag goes on a subject's entry, none on an author's, and no entry is made: a subject the lake does not hold has its link on the book side only (Neo Book) and gains it here at the first build after the lake holds it, with no change to the file. After changing the file, run `node lake/scripts/build.mjs --only bibliotheca` and then the catalogue (the dataset `bibliotheca-source-links`, and `get_entity`'s `source_links`).

## Refresh

`.github/workflows/lake-refresh.yml` rebuilds monthly (on the 3rd), tags every book again, counts the new tags (`--only bibliotheca`) and commits `data/`. Summaries are cached in `scripts/summary-cache.json`, so a refresh only fetches new articles.
