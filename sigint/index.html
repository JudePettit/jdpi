Connect your own receivers

Download prp-bridge.mjs

Decoders such as readsb, AIS-catcher, a Meshtastic radio or OpenWebRX publish JSON on your network, but a web page may only read it if the receiver allows it (CORS), and an HTTPS page may not load plain-HTTP addresses on your network at all. The PRP bridge is one small Node.js file with no dependencies that you run on your own computer: it listens on localhost only, fetches the endpoints you name (read-only), and passes them to this page with the right headers. It also relays the public aircraft feed and connects to aisstream.io for you, so your key never goes to a website.

  1. Install Node.js 22 or newer and download the bridge.
  2. Run it with your receivers’ addresses (built from the settings in each tab): 
    node prp-bridge.mjs
  3. Turn on Off
  4. The first time, Chrome and Edge ask to let this site “look for and connect to devices on your local network” (or “apps on this device”). Allow it for the bridge to work; you can withdraw it in the site settings.

Without the bridge, a receiver on localhost works directly if it sends CORS headers (AIS-catcher and Meshtastic do; readsb’s web server can be told to; see the ). Firefox and Safari block plain-HTTP addresses on your network from HTTPS pages: use the bridge there.

Interference maps

Derived from aircraft
GPSJamDaily map of where aircraft report degraded navigation accuracy, in hexagons: green under 2% of aircraft affected, yellow 2–10%, red over 10%. Updated after midnight UTC.Open at your location ↗ Flightradar24 GPS jamming mapThe same method from Flightradar24’s receivers, refreshed through the day, with a timeline.Open ↗

Airliners broadcast how accurate their satellite position is (the NACp field in ADS-B). When many aircraft over one area suddenly report poor accuracy, someone is jamming GNSS there. Spoofing, where false signals move the computed position, shows up differently: aircraft tracks jump or circle impossibly.

These maps cannot see where there are no aircraft, mix jamming with equipment faults at low percentages, and lag by hours to a day. Persistent interference has been widely reported around the Baltic Sea, the eastern Mediterranean, the Black Sea and parts of the Middle East: a useful indicator for conflict monitoring, never proof on its own of who is responsible.

Why only links: GPSJam’s data files don’t allow other websites to read them and carry no licence for re-use, so PRP sends you to the source instead of copying it.

APRS

144.800 MHz in Europe · 144.390 MHz in North America

The Automatic Packet Reporting System carries positions, weather and short messages from licensed radio amateurs, relayed by digipeaters and gated to the internet (APRS-IS). The public map shows it all.

Open aprs.fi at your location ↗

To decode packets yourself: an RTL-SDR, rtl_fm and Direwolf (see the ). Receiving needs no licence; gating to APRS-IS needs a callsign.

Refresh. Aircraft every 15 seconds (5 seconds from your own receiver, 30 seconds from OpenSky) while the tab is open and visible; ships every 10 seconds; space weather every 5 minutes; WSPR every 10 minutes; orbital elements at most every 6 hours (cached in this browser). Nothing is polled while the tab is hidden.

Method and limits. Public feeds show what volunteers’ receivers hear: coverage has holes, military and some private aircraft are filtered by the aggregators, and positions can be wrong. Pass times assume a flat horizon. Everything here is for awareness and the hobby, not navigation or safety of life.
