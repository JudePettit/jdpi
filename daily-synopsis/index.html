Daily Synopsis

Each day’s major political stories: those that at least three of the Political Briefing’s publishers, from more than one side, ran in the 24 hours. Under each, its 5 Sided Analysis lays out the outlets’ own headlines from far left to far right. A short written digest and comparison are added when an AI model’s draft passes Vitruvian Manager’s checks, and labelled as such.

        Sources. The Political Briefing’s store (briefing/data/latest.json, refreshed twice a day from fifty publishers, ten for each position), with the Scientific, Economics and Digital Briefings’ stores helping to group the headlines into stories, as Highbrowse groups them. Headlines and links belong to their publishers; each headline links to its article, which is the source of everything said about it here. The day’s files are daily-synopsis/data/days/, the input each draft was written from daily-synopsis/data/input/, and corrections daily-synopsis/data/corrections.json.

        Method. A story is a group of headlines that share enough of their rarer words (Highbrowse’s groups). It is a major story of the day when, in the 24 hours to the Political Briefing’s morning refresh, at least three different publishers ran a headline in it, from at least two of the five positions; the ten with the most publishers, then the most positions, are the day’s. Sport, entertainment and lifestyle are left out. Political positions are the Political Briefing’s (AllSides Media Bias Ratings where available, otherwise Media Bias/Fact Check, adjusted to five steps): they describe a publisher’s editorial slant, not its accuracy. ⚠ marks a publisher Media Bias/Fact Check rates mixed or low for factual reliability; beside it, “Wikipedia” and a status give the publisher’s row on English Wikipedia’s list of perennial sources, the standing consensus of Wikipedia’s editors, not Vitruvian Manager’s judgement. A written comparison is made only when at least three of the five sides carried the story, one left of centre and one right of centre. The check tests the text’s form and that its words, names and figures come from the reports it cites; it cannot test that a sentence is true to them.

        The written text. Once a day a scheduled run of an AI model (Claude) reads the day’s input file, and nothing else, and writes a draft. Vitruvian Manager’s rules check it before anything is shown: every sentence must cite the headlines it rests on, hold no quotation and no reported speech, and hold no number, date or name that is not in those headlines and their feeds’ short summaries; every other word must be in them too, or in a short list of common words such as “the”, “and” and “headline”; a name only one outlet carries is refused; no outlet or journalist is named; and the comparison states no motive or judgement. A draft that fails any rule is not shown at all, and the day keeps the outlets’ headlines alone. No person checks the text. A day is never rewritten once it has passed; a correction is a dated note shown beside it.
