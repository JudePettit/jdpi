What GEOINT and IMINT are

Geospatial intelligence (GEOINT) is what you learn by putting information on the surface of the Earth: where something is, how far and how big, what lies near it and how a place changes over time. Imagery intelligence (IMINT) is the part that comes from pictures of the Earth: satellite and aerial images, drone surveys and photographs taken on the ground.

For an open-source analyst they answer three questions. Where was this, when was it, and what changed? This dashboard brings together the free public data and the measuring tools to answer them, with guides to the software and the kit for going further.

    Ground rules

    • Use only what is public, without getting round logins, paywalls, CAPTCHAs or rate limits.
    • Investigate events, organisations, places, infrastructure and claims. Never locate, profile or track private people.
    • Check a finding three ways before you publish, and say how sure you are.
    • Think about who could be harmed by a map: people in danger, sources, and you.

        Sharper, commercial imagery

        Daily global images are coarse. For buildings and vehicles you need very-high-resolution (VHR) imagery, 30 cm to 3 m, mostly commercial and under licence:

        • Vantor Open Data (formerly Maxar): free sub-metre images of disasters, CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial, with credit), also on AWS.
        • Planet: PlanetScope about 3 m daily, SkySat about 50 cm. Paid; the Education and Research programme gives limited free access to university researchers. Licences restrict sharing the images.
        • Open radar: Umbra and Capella publish sample SAR scenes, CC BY 4.0.
        • Esri Wayback and Google Earth's historical imagery show dated VHR captures to look at, not to republish without permission.

        Always check the licence before you publish an image, credit the provider, and say what date it shows.

        Reading a hotspot

        A detection means a satellite saw heat in a pixel (375 m for VIIRS, 1 km for MODIS), not what caused it. Crop burning, wildfires, gas flares, steelworks and strikes all show. Cloud, smoke and the time between passes hide short fires.

        Compare the same place in earlier years, check the true-colour image for smoke, and look for the same heat on consecutive passes before you call it a fire. Lesson: tracking fires and lights →

            Sources. Imagery: NASA EOSDIS Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS), MODIS Terra and Aqua corrected reflectance (true colour and bands 7-2-1), VIIRS Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 corrected reflectance, VIIRS Black Marble (VNP46A2, moonlight-corrected and gap-filled) and the Day/Night Band, no restrictions on use, credit NASA. Fires: GIBS VIIRS and MODIS thermal anomalies (drawn by the GIBS WMS) and NASA FIRMS with your own free MAP_KEY. Earthquakes: USGS Earthquake Hazards Program GeoJSON feeds (public domain). Disaster alerts: GDACS, a cooperation of the United Nations and the European Commission. Features: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL) through the public Overpass API servers of Private.coffee or VK Maps; place search by Nominatim under the OSM Foundation's usage policy. Elevation: Terrain Tiles on AWS Open Data (Mapzen/Tilezen; SRTM, GMTED, ETOPO1, 3DEP, EU-DEM and other sources, see attribution). Base maps: Esri Canvas and World Imagery (Esri's terms of use apply), EOxCloudless Sentinel-2 2025 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Sun positions: SunCalc 2.1.1 (BSD-2-Clause); time zones: tz-lookup (CC0).

            Refresh. Everything is fetched live by your browser when you open a view: GIBS daily imagery appears about three hours after each satellite pass and the newest complete day is shown by default; FIRMS updates every 15 minutes; USGS feeds every minute; GDACS as events change. Nothing is stored on a PRP server.

            Method and privacy. Distances and bearings are computed on the WGS84 ellipsoid (Vincenty), areas on the equal-area sphere. Your FIRMS key is kept only in this browser's storage and sent only to firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov. Place searches go to nominatim.openstreetmap.org (one per second at most, never as you type), feature queries to the Overpass server you choose (Private.coffee unless you pick VK Maps), and identify this site by its address only. Use the tools for places, events and infrastructure, never to find or follow private people.
