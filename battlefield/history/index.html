Borders: Cliopatria v0.2.1 (Bennett et al. 2025), CC BY 4.0, processed by Vitruvian Manager; from 1946, a small state Cliopatria has no row for is drawn as Natural Earth’s point (public domain). Cliopatria ends in 2024, so 2025 is drawn on 2024’s borders.

    Method. A place is shaded red when a conflict with 1,000 or more battle deaths that year was fought on its territory, amber for 25–999 deaths, and violet when its forces fought only abroad. Before 1946 no free source codes the size of a war, so the wars on VM’s list from Wikidata have a colour of their own and no battle-death figure, and smaller conflicts are missing. Over a period of years, each place takes the most intense of its years and is drawn on the borders of the period’s last year; the lists include every conflict active in at least one year of the period. Borders are by the year, as Cliopatria draws them, and each place is a Wikidata item for the state of that year (the Soviet Union in 1950, Russia in 2000). A place VM cannot draw on a free border in a year is named under the map, never drawn on a border of today; a place Wikidata dates after that year is called “today’s” (today’s Malaysia in 1941), since it was not yet a state. The wars of 1926–1945 are placed where Wikidata names their country, a location that is a state or lies within one, or a participant; a region that spans several states (the Gran Chaco) is not spread to them. Their dates are Wikidata’s, to the day, the month or the year, as it gives them. A war that is part of a larger one on the list (the Continuation War, within World War II) is listed once, as the whole. The list and the crosswalk from Cliopatria’s rows to places are VM’s own work (battlefield/scripts/wars-1926.mjs and history-places.mjs).

    What these sources cannot show. VM’s own text. Borders are by the year, so a border that moved within a year is shown only as it stood in Cliopatria’s year. Before 1946 no free source codes a war’s type, each state’s years in it or its battle deaths, and Wikidata leaves some participants out (Canada, New Zealand and South Africa are not among World War II’s). Many places of the Second World War lay inside empires or occupied states that Cliopatria draws as one, so they are named under the map, not drawn.
