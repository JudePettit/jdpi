Sources. Facts from Wikidata (CC0). Summaries from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0; the text stays under that licence). Tags combine Wikidata facts with Vitruvian Manager’s own tags: schools of thought, ideologies and fields, in the discipline’s words, and a position on the line from left to right (the Political Briefing’s five), which is Vitruvian Manager’s own placement, from the Politics course, until a source is cited, and says so beside the tag. VM’s canon list adds the entries a reader of politics expects to find, each with the references that name it (Wikipedia’s vital articles and outlines, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, the standard textbooks); a tag it gives names its reference beside the tag.

Method. Every submodule reports the people, places, wars, parties and ideas it mentions; each is matched to its Wikidata ID, given tags from its facts, and linked back to the exact place it appears. Each entry has one type, read from its Wikidata classes: a polity is a state that no longer exists; a period, a named span of history; a site, an archaeological site, a monument or a building of historical note; a series, a grouping of other entries, such as a series of wars or the events of one year. An entry’s “Appears in Vitruvian Manager” counts those places, by module and submodule; a book is one place, and its line says how many times and in which chapters the book names the entry. A person’s name is shown without the realm Wikidata adds after a regnal number (“Frederik X”, not “Frederik X of Denmark”); the full label is kept among the other names. A book of Bibliotheca judged a primary or a secondary source on an entry says so on its line, with the evidence a click away; a work’s own entry lists the subjects it is a source on. The judgements are kept in one reviewed list, and the entry links to what its tags mean. Refreshed monthly.
