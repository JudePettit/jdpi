Omni-Map

In Neo Book, click a tagged name and VM opens a map on the places and years it can give that name, with borders as they stood: the passage's own years once VM holds a checked record of them, until then the dates of what was named. Each value shows its source and its date at the precision the source records, and 'Save this view' gives a bundle you can cite and download.

      This view

      What this is. A view is a recipe (VR/1): the years, the frame, the step, the layers and the places in focus, kept in this page's address, so a link draws it again. The address is a live view: it draws today's data. "Save this view (bundle)" freezes what was drawn: the recipe with its data versions, the rows, the features, the metadata and three citation files, with a pin anyone can check against its recipe, rows and features (the pin does not cover the other files). VM is not a primary source; cite the bundle, and through it the sources below.

      Borders. Borders: Cliopatria v0.2.1 (Bennett et al. 2025), CC BY 4.0 – processed by Vitruvian Manager. Ed Chalstrey and James Bennett, Cliopatria v0.2.1, Seshat Global History Databank, Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23128623; described in J. S. Bennett et al., 'Cliopatria - A geospatial database of world-wide political entities from 3400BCE to 2024CE', Scientific Data 12, 247 (2025), doi:10.1038/s41597-025-04516-9. Licence: CC BY 4.0. What Cliopatria says of itself: its README calls it a dataset of worldwide polities from 3400 BCE to 2024 CE, with over 1,600 political entities sampled at varying timesteps and spatial scales, and says its maps reflect only one version of the territory held by past polities; its paper says it draws a polity only where written sources attest to it and its extent, and that it does not encode border uncertainty or territorial disputes. These are a research dataset’s borders, made to compare polities across the world and five thousand years: they are coarser than a survey’s, and no line on this map is a surveyed boundary. Cliopatria v0.2.1 holds 13,797 rows, and fewer than 20 in any century before 800 BC, so the map shows few polities in those years. Cliopatria writes years before AD 1 as negative numbers; VM reads −1 as 1 BC, with no year 0. Changes by Vitruvian Manager: every row, 3400 BC to AD 2024, in files by years (a row spanning two files is in both), converted to TopoJSON quantised to 1/100,000 of each file’s extent (points that then coincide are merged; no simplification), with a row id added.

      Base. Made with Natural Earth. Natural Earth 1:110m land, version 5.1.1, public domain ("All versions of Natural Earth raster + vector map data found on this website are in the public domain", its terms, read 17 November 2026). Land only: before 1886 the base is never today's political map.

      Countries and population. Switched on in "Layers": today's countries from Natural Earth's Admin 0 – Countries, version 5.1.1 (public domain), drawn only from 1886; population density by country from the World Bank's World Development Indicators, EN.POP.DNST (CC BY 4.0), on today's borders; and population density in 0.25° cells summed by VM from GHSL's GHS-POP R2023A (European Commission, Joint Research Centre; Schiavina et al. 2023, doi:10.2905/2FF68A52-5B5B-4A22-8F40-C41DA8332CFE; CC BY 4.0), 1975–2030, of which 2025 and 2030 are projections. Each layer's legend, source, licence and method are in its line.

      Battles. Wikidata (CC0), with each value's item and property: the battles the Master Index holds with a date in 1400–1600, their dates at the precision and in the calendar Wikidata records (never converted), their points as Wikidata gives them, and what supports each value ("Wikipedia-sourced, unverified" where a value was only imported from a Wikipedia; "no reference" where nothing supports it).

      The map. Map drawn with MapLibre GL JS 5.24.0 (BSD 3-Clause, © MapLibre contributors; "Redistribution and use in source and binary forms, with or without modification, are permitted", its licence, read 17 November 2026), loaded from cdn.jsdelivr.net at that exact version with its Subresource Integrity hash, so a changed file is refused. Without it, or without WebGL, the page says so and lists what the map would show.

      Method. The legend's ⓘ holds each layer's method note. The click's rules (which years, which frame, which step, which places) are set out in omni/README.md, "How a click sets the view": the years are the passage's only where its periods record has been checked; otherwise they are the dates of what was named, widened by 10%, and the frame is the start of the book's era. A place whose dates do not hold the frame is left out, never drawn as a present-day state, and one VM cannot outline is named with the reason.
