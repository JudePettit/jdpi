    Method. A place is shaded red when a conflict with 1,000 or more battle deaths that year was fought on its territory, amber for 25–999 deaths, and violet when its forces fought only abroad. Before 1946 no free source codes the size of a war, so the wars on VM’s list from Wikidata have a colour of their own and no battle-death figure, and smaller conflicts are missing. Over a period of years, each place takes the most intense of its years and is drawn on the borders of the period’s last year; the lists include every conflict active in at least one year of the period. Borders are by the year, as Cliopatria draws them, and each place is a Wikidata item for the state of that year (the Soviet Union in 1950, Russia in 2000). Yugoslavia is one item for all its years and is named by the year: the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes to 1929, the Kingdom of Yugoslavia to 1945 and the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia after, each Wikidata’s name for the state of those years. In the same way, and in every view (the map, the table, a war’s panel, a country’s heading), China is the Republic of China to 1949, Saudi Arabia the Kingdom of Nejd and Hejaz to 1932 and Ethiopia the Ethiopian Empire to 1975, as are Iraq, Lebanon, Malaysia, Oman, Sri Lanka, Tunisia, Somaliland, South Yemen and North Yemen by their earlier states’ names (a protectorate that Wikidata also calls a historical country counts as a state: the French protectorate of Tunisia to 1956), each name and its last year from that state’s own Wikidata item. The year of a change keeps the earlier name, since the year began with it (the State of Greater Lebanon in 1926, the Malayan Union in 1948). A place VM cannot draw on a free border in a year is named under the map, never drawn on a border of today; a place named for a year before the state of that name existed is called “today’s” (today’s Malaysia in 1941, today’s Algeria in 1955), since it was a colony or a territory then, and a state that is gone is never called “today’s”. Kuwait is not: Wikidata dates its item from the liberation of 1991, but shows it a sovereign state from 1961. Western Sahara, whose Wikidata item gives no inception, is “today’s Western Sahara” to 1975, since Wikidata ends the country that held it, Spanish Sahara, in 1976. From 1946 a conflict’s type is UCDP’s for each year, since UCDP types every year apart (Cuba’s war was a civil conflict in 1953 and 1956–58 and an internationalised one in 1961); over a period the table gives every type it had then. Its Active span runs from its first to its last year in UCDP’s record, with the number of years it was active, since the record can have gaps (Nigeria: Biafra, 1967–70 and 2021–25, is “1967–ongoing · 9 yrs”). UCDP’s location is the government side of a conflict, not where the fighting was (UCDP’s codebook), so VM places some conflicts by their fighting (battlefield/scripts/history-fought.mjs): an interstate war where it was fought, a government’s war that reached abroad by the countries of its fighting (from 1989, UCDP’s georeferenced events), and the internal conflicts fought outside the government’s state where Wikidata places the war: Israel: Palestine in Palestine and Israel, South Africa: Namibia in Namibia, Morocco’s and Mauritania’s wars over Western Sahara in Western Sahara and in the government’s state, which Polisario’s raids reached, and Indonesia: East Timor in Timor-Leste. The map draws Namibia within South Africa to 1989 and Timor-Leste within Indonesia from 1976, so each is named under the map, and the shape that takes it in counts that conflict as fighting abroad. Every other internal conflict is placed at UCDP’s location, the government’s state. The wars of 1926–1945 are placed where Wikidata names their country, a location that is a state or lies within one, or a participant; a region that spans several states (the Gran Chaco) is not spread to them. Their dates are Wikidata’s, to the day, the month or the year, as it gives them. A war that is part of a larger one on the list (the Continuation War, within World War II) is listed once, as the whole, except War Focus’s own wars, which are listed on their own even inside a larger war on the list: the Northern Expedition (within the Chinese Civil War), the Japanese invasion of Manchuria (within the Second Sino-Japanese War), the Second Sino-Japanese War and the Winter War (within World War II) and the Battles of Khalkhin Gol (within the Soviet–Japanese border conflicts). The list and the crosswalk from Cliopatria’s rows to places are VM’s own work (battlefield/scripts/wars-1926.mjs and history-places.mjs).

    Method: the charts above the table. VM’s own text. The first counts each year’s armed conflicts in UCDP/PRIO’s record by the type UCDP gives the conflict in that year, from 1946: civil (UCDP’s intrastate), internationalised civil (internationalised intrastate), interstate, or colonial (extrasystemic), stacked from the bottom in that order; a conflict whose type changed is counted under each year’s own. UCDP’s coding starts in 1946, and Wikidata names the wars before it only by their kind, so 1926–1945 is drawn as one strip marked “not coded”, never as zero. Each conflict counts once, whatever its size. The second gives the battle deaths of each year from 1989, the first year of UCDP’s free series, on a time scale of its own (a dashed line in the first marks where it begins): UCDP’s best estimate as a line, and as its band the sum of the conflicts’ low estimates to the sum of their high ones. They are battle deaths in state-based conflicts only; UCDP keeps one-sided violence and non-state violence in other datasets, not drawn here. Both follow the timeline: its year or period is shaded, and a period fades the bars outside it. “The figures, year by year” gives every number the charts draw.

    What these sources cannot show. VM’s own text. Borders are by the year, so a border that moved within a year is shown only as it stood in Cliopatria’s year. Before 1946 no free source codes a war’s type, each state’s years in it or its battle deaths, and Wikidata leaves some participants out (Canada, New Zealand and South Africa are not among World War II’s). Many places of the Second World War lay inside empires or occupied states that Cliopatria draws as one, so they are named under the map, not drawn.
