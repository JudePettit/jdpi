Sources. Energy capture per person: Ian Morris, Why the West Rules — For Now (2010) and The Measure of Civilization (2013), following the shape of his Western and Eastern core series. Populations: McEvedy & Jones, Maddison and standard estimates. From 1900: primary-energy statistics (Energy Institute / Our World in Data), with food and traditional biomass added. Rating: Carl Sagan's K = (log₁₀ P − 6) ÷ 10, with P in watts.

Method. A power's share is its population × energy per person, divided by the world total. Before 1800, treat shares as accurate to within ±30–50%; some entries combine several states. Steps are a century apart until 1800, then 1850, 1900, 1950, 2000 and 2025. A period is every step from its first to its last, both included: the map shows each power that was among the powers at any of them, sized by its largest share at any one step (named with its year), and nothing is added up or averaged across steps. Its two ends are compared too: each power's share at the first and last steps, the change in percentage points, and regional totals, which hold up better than single powers whose borders and names changed in between.
