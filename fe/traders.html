Sources. Members of Congress: Periodic Transaction Reports filed with the Clerk of the US House of Representatives (this year and last). Famous investors: Form 13F-HR filings on SEC EDGAR, the latest two per manager. Tickers: OpenFIGI, which maps each holding's CUSIP to its US listing. All refreshed weekly.

Members of Congress. Under the STOCK Act (2012), members of the House must report stock, bond and option trades by themselves, their spouses, joint accounts and dependent children within 45 days. Amounts are ranges ($1,001–$15,000 up to over $50 million), not exact values; some members file late; scanned paper filings are linked but not read. The Senate publishes its reports only behind a terms-of-use page on efdsearch.senate.gov, so Senators are not included yet.

Famous investors. Managers with more than $100 million in US equities file Form 13F within 45 days of each quarter end. It lists long positions in US-listed shares and options at the quarter end, but not short positions, cash, most non-US holdings or trades within the quarter. Changes compare the latest two filings. A filing marked "old" means the manager has not filed recently, for example because the fund closed or now files under another name.

Commentators. Television and social-media commentators such as Jim Cramer have no duty to disclose, so there is no official record of their picks; this page sticks to legally filed disclosures.

Not investment advice. Disclosures can be late, amended or incomplete; check the linked filing. Agents in Algo Trader simulate before they trade.
